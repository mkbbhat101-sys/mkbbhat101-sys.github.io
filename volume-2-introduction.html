Kashmir and Beyond • Volume II • In progress

Introduction

A running collection of passages for Volume II, added as material comes in from the September 2026 trip and beyond. No structure fixed yet; the material will dictate it once there's enough of it on the table.

I did not plan this sequel the way a writer plans a sequel, with an outline waiting at the start for events to fill in. Kashmir and Beyond was always going to be a book about my own displacement, finished because a book has to end somewhere even when the thing it describes does not. This second volume began instead as exactly what the page before this one calls it: a running record, dated passage by dated passage, kept as the actual days of a particular return unfolded rather than reconstructed afterward from memory alone. I did not know, when I wrote the first of these passages on the fifteenth of September 2026, how many more would follow it, or where the last one would land. I know now, having written all of them, that I still do not fully know what the whole adds up to. I offer it to you anyway, in the order it arrived in me, because I think that order is itself part of what I am trying to tell you.

In September 2026, thirty-six years after my family was made to leave Chandpora, I went back. I reached Jammu by a train that did not exist the last time any part of this journey was open to me, and from there I travelled by road into the Valley, climbing toward Kashmir rather than dropping into it from the sky, for the first time since 1990. What follows is not a travelogue in the ordinary sense, though it will read like one in places, temples climbed, springs stood before, meals eaten, drives taken. It is closer to an accounting, kept in something near real time, of what it actually feels like to return to a place that was once simply yours and is now, by every practical measure, someone else's, while remaining, by every measure that matters less to a lawyer and more to a son, still entirely your own.

— Manoj Bhat
